1What Mumble is

In plain terms

Mumble lets you talk instead of type. Press one shortcut, speak, and Mumble writes out clean text wherever your cursor is — in an email, a chat box, a document, anywhere. The speech-to-text happens on your own machine (private), and an optional AI step can polish or reshape the result if you want it.

One-liner: Mumble is a voice-first writing tool for Windows (with macOS and Linux editions) that turns what you say into polished, ready-to-paste text anywhere on your computer.

Elevator pitch: You press a global shortcut, speak naturally, and Mumble transcribes your voice locally (the audio never leaves your machine), then optionally runs it through an AI to clean it up, restructure it, draft an email, write a reply, translate it, summarise it — whatever the moment calls for. The result lands at your cursor in whatever app you're in: your email client, a chat box, a document, a code editor. A small floating island shows you what's happening; a permanently available Deck keeps every transcript, clipboard item and saved prompt one click away.

The long version. Mumble lives in your system tray and overlays the rest of your desktop. Its philosophy (internally called "The Big Shift") is you should think less; Mumble should infer more — instead of memorising modes and commands, you just talk, and Mumble figures out whether you wanted a tidy paragraph, a bulleted list, an email, or a reply to the thing you have highlighted. It is offline-first for transcription (speech-to-text runs on-device), and bring-your-own-AI for the optional polish step (you choose the provider/model, or run a local model). Everything you produce is stored locally and is browsable, searchable, favouritable, and re-runnable from the Deck.

What makes it distinctive (for ads)

  • Speak anywhere, paste anywhere — it's not an app you write inside; it injects text into whatever app already has your cursor.
  • Local transcription — privacy as a first-class feature ("Audio transcribed locally").
  • AI is optional and yours — connect Cerebras / OpenAI / Anthropic / DeepSeek / Groq, or run local.
  • A gorgeous black-and-gold "liquid glass" interface with restrained, premium motion (sheen/shimmer on hover, a breathing island).
  • The Deck — a pinned, always-there palette of everything you've said and copied.

3The Design System — single source of truth

In plain terms

This section is the heart of copying Mumble's look. The aesthetic is called "Golden Black": a near-black, warm, dark interface with a single brand accent — gold — plus a small set of functional accent colours that each have a job. Surfaces look like liquid glass. Motion is a quiet, classy sheen of light that sits or sweeps across the glass.

Below are the exact colour values, fonts, glass recipes, and the shimmer rules. The colour numbers (hex codes like #D4AF37) are the real ones from the product — match them precisely.

This is the heart of replicating Mumble. The aesthetic name is "Golden Black" — a near-black, warm, dark UI with a single brand accent (gold) and a small set of functional accent colors. Surfaces are liquid glass. Motion is a quiet specular sheen/shimmer.

3.1 Core principle — Black & Gold only

Shades of black and gold are unlimited. Every other color is a functional highlight with a job: the per-mode accent colors, and status red/amber/green. No other decorative colors (no burgundy, no random blues except the functional email-mode blue).

3.4 Liquid glass — two recipes

In plain terms

Mumble fakes the "frosted glass" look two ways. Real glass uses an actual blur of whatever is behind it — pretty but expensive for the computer, so it's used sparingly on small floating bits. Fake glass is the everyday card: a near-solid gold-tinted gradient with a thin highlight and a shadow, no blur at all — it looks the same on top of dark content but costs the GPU almost nothing.

Mumble uses two kinds of "glass" for performance and depth:

A) REAL glass — actual backdrop-filter blur. Reserved for small chrome over content (nav pill, status chip, the island, the Deck panel). Budget: ≈6–8 blurred elements per screen max.

Code .glass — real backdrop-blur glass 
.glass {
  background: linear-gradient(145deg, rgba(40,37,30,0.55), rgba(20,19,16,0.55));
  backdrop-filter: blur(18px) saturate(150%);
  -webkit-backdrop-filter: blur(18px) saturate(150%);
  border: 1px solid rgba(245,238,222,0.08);
  box-shadow: var(--shadow-sm), var(--glass-hi);
}

B) FAKE glass — the workhorse card. Near-opaque gold-tinted gradient + inset highlight + shadow, no blur (invisible behind 90%+ opacity, saves ~80% GPU). This is what most surfaces use.

3.5 SHIMMER — the signature motion (READ CAREFULLY)

In plain terms

The "shimmer" is the single most important motion detail, and it's easy to get wrong. It is not constant glitter. At rest, a faint band of light just sits still on each glass surface — parked, not moving — so nothing ever looks out of sync. Synced stillness reads as "crafted"; random twinkling reads as "broken."

The light only moves on hover: a single bright band sweeps across once, fast, and exits the edge — it never loops and never gets stuck. Buttons get a quick diagonal glint; the active navigation tab has a slow continuous shine; the big hero headline has its own travelling gold sheen.

Mumble's shimmer is not a constant glitter. The contract:

  • At rest: a faint specular sheen sits statically on each glass surface (a diagonal light band parked off-centre). It does not animate — so no two widgets are ever out of sync (synced-stillness reads as "crafted," random twinkling reads as "broken").
  • On hover: a bright light band sweeps across once, fast, then exits off the element (it does not loop, does not get "stuck").
  • Buttons/sub-tabs: a quick diagonal glint sweeps across on hover.
  • The active nav tab: a slow shine travels across it continuously.
  • The hero headline only: an autonomous travelling gold sheen on the gold text.

3.6 Visual tiers

Mumble ships three visual tiers the user can pick (so ads can show the richest):

  • Basic / Standard — fake glass, static sheen, hover sweeps. Crisp, low-GPU.
  • Enhanced (the showcase — use this for ads) — real glass in more places, ambient gold "dust" particles, a breathing hero glow, drifting blobs, glowing chart bars, the works. Body gets class enhanced.
  • Lite / Resource-Saver — strips animation, blur, shadows for low-end machines.

For advertising, always depict Enhanced: it's the most beautiful and on-brand.

3.7 Typography

  • UI font: Segoe UI / Inter / system sans. Tight letter-spacing on headings (-0.01em to -0.02em).
  • Mono font: Cascadia Code / Consolas — used for keycaps and code.
  • Brand name "Mumble": gold, weight 700, ~20px, letter-spacing: -0.02em.
  • Page titles: ~21px, weight 700, --text color.
  • Body: 12.5–13px, --text-dim, line-height ~1.5.

5The Floating Island (signature visual)

In plain terms

The "Island" is Mumble's most recognisable element: a small glass pill that floats on top of everything on your screen while you dictate or while the AI works. It's see-through and click-through (it floats like a heads-up display), it glows gold, and it changes colour and label to show what's happening — Listening, Transcribing, drafting an Email, and so on. For ads, lead with this.

The Island is Mumble's most recognisable element: a small, floating, always-on-top liquid-glass capsule (pill) that appears over everything else on the desktop while you dictate or while AI is working. It is click-through and per-pixel transparent (a layered window), so it hovers like a HUD.

Exact look

  • A rounded capsule (full pill ends), ~34px tall, min ~118px wide, growing with content. Centred in a ~460×64 canvas with room for its glow.
  • Warm glass body: vertical gradient from #1B1813 (top) to #0B0A09 (bottom).
  • Rim: a warm 1px hairline (#3A3320) with an inner top highlight (#6A6047).
  • A soft drop-shadow beneath and a gold glow halo around it.
  • A breathing gold rim, a slow travelling sheen across the glass, a ripple dot, an audio-reactive waveform, bouncing dots (thinking), and a shimmer text label.
  • Per-state accent color drives the rim/dot/wave — using the same mode palette as §3.2.

16Motion & animation language

In plain terms

Mumble's motion is restrained and premium. Almost everything uses a springy easing curve with a tiny overshoot. At rest, very little moves — only the Island's breathing pulse and the live waveform. The interface comes alive on hover: cards lift and a light band sweeps across once. Never animate blur (it's slow to paint); animate only position and opacity.

  • Easing: almost everything uses the spring cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1) — a slight overshoot that feels lively but premium.
  • Idle motion is minimal: only the island's breathing pulse + the live recording waveform move at rest. Cards' sheen is parked (static). (In Enhanced tier, add: ambient gold dust, a breathing hero glow, drifting blobs, the today-bar pulse, the slow nav-tab shine.)
  • Hover is where it comes alive: cards lift 3px + gain a gold edge-glow + a one-shot bright sheen sweeps across; buttons glint; rows lift 1px and gold-edge.
  • Entrances: views fade-up 8px; modals/Deck scale-in from 98.5% with a 10px rise (premIn).
  • Confirmations: a "saved ring" pulse; selected items "pop" (scale 0.95→1.05→1).
  • Never animate filter/backdrop-filter (paint-bound). Animate transform/opacity only.

Keyframes worth reusing (also in §3.5): pulse, glowPulse, hoverSweep, btnSheen, navShine, goldSheen, premIn, viewIn, the wave set waveA–E.

17How to build the ad carousels

In plain terms

Rules of thumb for making the ads: keep it dark, lead with the Island or the glowing waveform (the most recognisable bits), let gold be the hero and use mode colours only as labels, depict the richest "Enhanced" tier, and give it lots of calm negative space. Below are a suggested nine-card carousel and a do/don't list.

Format & framing

  • Always dark (#0A0A0B base with the two faint gold corner glows). Never put Mumble UI on a light background.
  • Lead with the island or the hero waveform — they are the most recognisable, most "alive" elements.
  • Gold is the hero accent; use mode colors only to label modes (email = blue, reply = coral, list = teal, prompt = purple, foreign = sand). Don't introduce off-palette colors.
  • Use Enhanced tier visuals (richest glass + glow).
  • Generous negative space; the UI is a tall, calm column.

  • ❌ Don't depict the audio going to the cloud (transcription is local).
